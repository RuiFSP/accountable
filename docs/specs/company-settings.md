# Spec: Company settings

- **Company settings**: the business's own details for the documents it sends. One page, opened from the menu under your initials, holds them for the whole team: company name, address, email and phone, tax label and rate, payment terms in days, and the prefixes for quote and invoice numbers; below them a Documents section holds how its invoices look — one of three designs, an accent color and the company's logo. Anyone signed in can open the page and save changes; everyone sees the same values.
- **The fields**: the company name is required; a name of only spaces is refused. The rest can be blank. The address is one free-text block, a filled email must look like an email, and the phone is free text. A blank tax rate or payment terms value saves and shows as 0. The tax rate is a percentage from 0 to 100 with up to three decimal places, such as 8.875%; the tax label is free text, such as Sales tax or VAT. Payment terms are a whole number of days from 0 to 365. Each prefix is at most 10 characters of letters, digits and hyphens, such as QT- and INV-.
- **Checking**: the page and the backend apply the same rules. A value that is out of range or in the wrong shape — a tax rate of 150%, payment terms of 400 days, an 11-character prefix — is refused with a clear message, through the page or straight to the API, and nothing invalid is stored.
- **Last change**: the page shows who last changed the settings and when, as their email and a date and time such as "Nov 10, 2026 · 9:30 AM". A save that changes nothing leaves that line as it was.
- **Documents**: the name, address, email and phone make the letterhead, the tax and payment terms work out the invoice, the invoice prefix starts its numbers, and the design, accent color and logo decide how the invoice looks — on its preview and on its PDF. The Documents section previews a sample invoice that updates as you choose. Quotes and bills come later and will use the same details.

## Design decisions

Points this spec fixes:

14. **One row for the team.** One `company_settings` row holds the settings for everyone; `GET /api/settings` returns it and `PUT /api/settings` replaces it. There is no per-user copy and no second row.
15. **Settings sit in the user menu.** The page is at `/settings` and opens from the menu under your initials, with the theme choice and signing out. Settings stays out of the top bar's seven sections.
16. **The tax rate stores three decimal places exactly.** `tax_rate_thousandths` stores the rate as an integer number of thousandths of a percent: 8.875% is 8875, 100% is 100000 and 0 is 0. SQLite and Postgres hold the same value, and the page shows the rate without rounding.
17. **Saving records who and when.** `updated_by` holds the signed-in user's email and `updated_at` the moment of the change; both move only when a stored value differs. The page shows them together, in the reader's local time.
18. **A database without settings starts as Brightloop.** Brightloop is the business the sample team works for: 1 Brightloop Plaza, Floor 12, New York, NY 10004; hello@brightloop.example; +1 555 0100; Sales tax at 8.875%; payment terms of 30 days; prefixes QT- and INV-, with the Classic design, the Midnight accent and its logo. A database with no settings row starts with these values, with a made-up teammate as the last person to change them, and resetting the sample data reloads them. A database whose settings predate the Documents section keeps what it has and gains the Classic design, the Midnight accent and no logo.
19. **Quote and invoice numbers.** Each kind has its own count in `number_counters`, starting at 1, and a number is its prefix as the settings have it when the number is taken, then the next count to at least four figures: QT-0001, then QT-0002, and QT-10000 after QT-9999. The count is taken inside the document's own transaction, in one atomic step so two saves at once never repeat a number, and a failed save rolls its number back so none is skipped. A reset clears the counts. Invoices take their number when they are issued and keep it forever; a voided invoice's number stays used.
20. **The sums on a document.** A line's amount is its quantity times its unit price, in whole cents; the subtotal adds the lines; the tax is worked out once on the subtotal at the settings' rate, to the nearest cent with halves up; the total is the subtotal plus the tax. The backend works out what it saves and never trusts totals from the browser. The page's live preview uses the same rules in `frontend/src/lib/tax.ts`, and both sides run `shared/totals-cases.json`. A quantity is a whole number from 1 to 1,000,000, and a line and the subtotal stay within the money columns' limit. Invoices use these sums throughout.
21. **The Documents section saves apart.** Below the company details, the Documents section holds the invoice design — Classic, Modern or Compact — the accent color and the logo, with its own Save, because its sample preview updates as you choose and the logo is a file. Both saves move the one last-change line. These choices are the team's current look for every invoice's preview and PDF, issued invoices included, while an issued invoice keeps the tax label and rate it was issued with.
22. **The accent is checked for contrast.** The accent is a hex color such as `#073B4C`, and the page and the backend both refuse one below 4.5:1 against white, with a clear message, so text on the document stays readable. The default is Midnight `#073B4C`, the brand's ink. The accent colors the document only; the app around it keeps the brand tokens and gains no new color.
23. **The logo is an image in the database.** A PNG or JPEG of up to 1 MB, and the backend checks that the file really is an image of that type, whatever its name says, and refuses anything else, SVG included. It is kept in the `company_settings` row, since Render's disk doesn't keep files, and served back at its own address for the page and the PDF. With no logo the letterhead shows the business name alone, and the section can remove an uploaded one.
