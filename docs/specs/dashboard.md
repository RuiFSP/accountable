# Spec: Dashboard

- **Dashboard**, the landing page: how sales are going at a glance. Deals won and revenue won month by month over the last 12 months, the open pipeline and its expected revenue (each deal's value times its probability of close), a sales funnel showing how much value reaches each stage, recent activity, and the follow-ups that are coming up or overdue. Below the sales charts, one Money section shows both sides: what customers owe, how much of it is overdue and how late, and the payments received each month; and what the business owes its suppliers — what is still unpaid, how much is overdue, the bills due in the next 30 days and spending by category over the last 12 months. It is visually striking, with at least a line chart, a bar chart and a visual of the pipeline.

## Design decisions

Points this spec fixes:

5. **Dashboard charts.** The dashboard shows a line chart (revenue won per month), a bar chart (deals won per month) and a funnel built from stage bars. The figures must match the data.
6. **The Money section.** Below the sales charts and above recent activity, it shows both halves in one section. What customers owe is every issued invoice's balance — drafts and void invoices left out — how much of that is overdue and how late it is, in five buckets: not yet due, 1 to 30, 31 to 60, 61 to 90 and over 90 days past due, where an invoice due today is not yet due. Below those, the payments received in each of the last 12 months, bucketed by the day the payment arrived, in the same 12 buckets every other chart uses. What the business owes its suppliers sits beside it: "Bills to pay" is the total of every unpaid bill, tax included, and how many bills that is; "Overdue" is the total and count of the unpaid bills whose due date is before today; "Due in the next 30 days" is the total and count of the unpaid bills due from today through 30 days from today. The three figures match the Bills tab: "Bills to pay" covers its Unpaid and Overdue views together, "Overdue" is its Overdue view, and the 30-day figure is the bills under the timeline's dates. A paid bill is in none of the three. Every figure comes from the invoices, payments and bills themselves and must match them.
7. **The timeline and the spending chart.** The timeline lists the bills due in the next 30 days, soonest first, each with its supplier, its due date and its total, and says when nothing is due. The spending chart covers the same 12 months as the sales charts, one stacked bar per month with each category a segment in its category's color, counting each paid bill's total in the month it was paid. Unpaid bills are left out of the chart.
8. **One section, two halves.** The customers' half shows two KPI cards — what is owed and how much is overdue — with the aging as bars and the payments as a line chart, in the brand's chart colors and JetBrains Mono figures. The suppliers' half shows its three figures, the due timeline and the stacked spending chart. Both sit under the one Money heading, the customers first.
