# Spec: Bills

- **Bills**: the Bills tab in Money, at `/money/bills`, records the bills the business receives from suppliers. The table shows each bill's supplier, the supplier's bill number, the bill date, the due date, the category, the total, the status and — for a paid bill — the day it was paid, with a status filter whose Unpaid, Overdue and Paid views divide every bill — Unpaid is the bills not yet due, Overdue the ones past their due date, and Paid the ones already paid — and a search box that matches the supplier's name or the bill number. Add and edit happen in a dialog, and a row's actions mark the bill paid, mark it unpaid again, edit it or delete it. Each category shows in its own color, and an unpaid bill past its due date shows as overdue.
- **Bill**: money the business owes a supplier. The supplier, which is an organization, the supplier's own bill number, the bill date, the due date, the amount before tax, the tax as it appears on the supplier's bill, a category and notes. A bill is unpaid or paid, and a paid bill carries the day it was paid. The total is the amount plus the tax, worked out by the backend. The due date starts 30 days after the bill date and can be changed. The same supplier can't have two bills with the same bill number. Every bill records who added it and who last changed it, as their email, and each organization's page shows the bills it supplied.

## Design decisions

Points this spec fixes:

1. **A supplier is an organization.** A bill points at one organization chosen from the list; adding a supplier is done in Organizations. Organizations carry no customer or supplier flag, so the same company can be a customer, a supplier or both. A bill must have a supplier, and deleting the supplier organization is refused while bills point at it.
2. **The supplier's tax, not ours.** The amount before tax and the tax are typed as they appear on the supplier's bill and stored as whole cents, zero or more and within what the money columns hold. The company settings' tax rate is never used. The total is the amount plus the tax, worked out and stored by the backend, never taken from the browser.
3. **The due date starts 30 days out.** Adding a bill sets its due date 30 days after the bill date, and the due date moves when the bill date changes until it is changed by hand. It can be any date. A bill is overdue when it is unpaid and its due date is before today; a bill due today is unpaid, not overdue.
4. **Paid or unpaid, with the day it was paid.** `paid_date` holds the day and is empty while a bill is unpaid. Marking paid records the day it was paid, defaulting to today; marking unpaid is one click and clears the date. A paid bill is never overdue, and editing a bill never changes whether it is paid. Both actions update the list, the organization's bills panel and the dashboard's figures at once.
5. **One bill number per supplier.** The bill number is required and trimmed, and the same supplier can't have two bills with the same number, ignoring case. The rule is checked on create and on update in Python, so SQLite and Postgres answer the same, and the form shows the message the API returns.
6. **Categories are fixed, each in its own color.** Every bill has exactly one category — Software, Contractors, Travel, Office, Marketing or Other — and the list shows it as in [Look and feel](look-and-feel.md). The color sits in a dot beside the category's name, never instead of the name. There is no category list to edit.
7. **Bills live in the tab and on the organization's page.** A bill has no detail page: the tab's table and dialog carry everything. The organization's page gains a bills panel below contacts and deals through the shared panel slot, listing that supplier's bills with their number, due date, total and status, and the page never names the area that made the panel. The panel is read-only; marking paid, editing and deleting happen in the Bills tab.
8. **The row shows the total, the dialog shows the amounts.** The table shows each bill's total, in US dollars with a dollar sign in JetBrains Mono, as money is shown everywhere else. The amount before tax, the tax and the notes are in the add and edit dialog, so the row keeps its shape on a 1024px screen. The dialog also shows who added the bill and who last changed it, since a bill has no detail page.
9. **A bill can be deleted.** Delete asks first and removes the bill; a bill holds no other records, so nothing else goes with it. The list and the dashboard's figures follow at once.
